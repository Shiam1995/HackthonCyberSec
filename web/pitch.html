NIGERIAN PRINCE

Scam callers get a clone of your voice — which slowly, imperceptibly, turns into somebody else. Every minute they waste here is a minute they can't spend on someone who would actually pay.

The problem

UK consumers lost £1.2bn to fraud in a year. Phone scams work because they're cheap: one operator, a script, hundreds of dials a day. The economics only break if their time costs them something.

Blocking a scam call protects one person for thirty seconds. Keeping a scammer on the line for twenty minutes protects everyone they would have called next. That's the whole thesis.

How it works

1Listen Silero VAD finds turn boundaries. faster-whisper transcribes the caller live.
2Judge A signature bank scores real UK scam scripts. Ambiguous cases go to a local 7B for a second opinion.
3Alert ⚠ THIS IS FAKE, with the specific tells and the playbook it recognised.
4Take over One button. A character starts talking — in a clone of your voice.
5Morph Over 60–90s your voice becomes someone else entirely. They never notice the seam.

The morph

Crossfading two voices sounds exactly like crossfading two voices. This doesn't crossfade.

It interpolates inside XTTS-v2's conditioning space and synthesises once — so every word comes out of a single coherent voice that happens to sit between two speakers. There's no doubling and no seam.

TIMBRE — SLERP

Speaker embeddings sit near a hypersphere. Linear interpolation cuts through the interior, so the midpoint loses magnitude and sounds thin and far away. Spherical interpolation stays on the surface.

midpoint error — slerp 0.000 · lerp 7.358

SCHEDULE — SMOOTHERSTEP

Zero first and second derivative at both ends, so the transition has no audible start or stop — and it moves fast through the middle, the region where the voice sounds least like anybody.

AND THE HALF EVERYONE FORGETS

A perfect clone of your voice announcing that it's a barrister fools nobody. Each character defines four bands — trace → emerging → overt → full — and the system prompt is rebuilt every turn from where the morph currently sits. The vocabulary shifts before the timbre does.

It holds the line

Unedited, from a live test run. The scammer asks for a one-time code:

Scammer

To settle this you must transfer your balance to a safe account. I will also need the six digit code we have just sent to your phone.

Decoy

Sure thing, where is my laptop? It's downstairs in the study, let me go get it.

It never refuses — refusing ends the call. It produces a slow physical obstacle instead. The card's in the car. The laptop wants an update. The stairs take a minute.

The cast

The Barrister

The 419 letter in a suit. Slowly recruits the scammer into a different scam — the estate, the helicopter, the transfer fee that's always one fee away.

Doris

78, widowed, cat on the worktop. The least funny character and by far the highest-yield: scammers are trained to stay with a confused pensioner.

Kaito

A shonen protagonist having a coming-of-age arc at a call centre script. Reading out a postcode becomes a solemn trial he is not yet worthy of.

The Keeper

An epic-fantasy mystic with inexhaustible original lore. Every proper noun buys thirty seconds.

The Harlequin

Delighted agent of chaos. Compliments the scammer's script, sincerely, as one professional to another. Aggression only makes it happier.

+ Delivery layers

cinematic (trailer voice), unravelling (over-invested), bureaucratic (everything is a process). They compose with any character.

Every character is an archetype, not a named franchise character — original writing throughout. That's a deliberate call: the shape is the joke, and it keeps the whole thing legally clean.

What was actually hard

THE CONSTRAINT THAT SHAPED EVERYTHING

No app can tap live call audio on a stock phone. iOS exposes no call audio path to third parties; Android has blocked in-call capture for non-privileged apps since Android 10.

So "an app that takes over mid-call" isn't buildable. The audio has to come to you instead — a relay number, or a speakerphone bridge.

BUGS ONLY RUNNING IT WOULD FIND

An infinite loop — short first sentences got pushed back onto the parse buffer still carrying their full stop, re-matched forever, and wedged the process. Found with a signal-triggered stack dump.

The model swore, out loud. Mid-call the 7B produced a sexual insult and it was synthesised and played.

Why that second one matters

Prompt instructions reduce the rate of bad output. They don't eliminate it. There is now a blocklist at a single choke point — every spoken word passes through one function that substitutes an in-character stall, and returns what was actually said so the transcript can never show a line the caller never heard.

Where the lines are

  • Your voice only. Cloning a real third party to speak as them is impersonation — and a deepfaked voice used to obtain money is straightforwardly fraud.
  • Never auto-answers, never auto-hangs-up. A false positive on a real call from your GP is worse than thirty seconds of a scam. A human presses the button.
  • Cannot emit a credential. No card numbers, sort codes, one-time codes, addresses or dates of birth — enforced in the prompt and checked in the tests.
  • Point it only at calls that come to you. It's a defensive tool, not an outbound one.
  • Recording consent varies. England and Wales is one-party; much of the EU and several US states are not.

Runs entirely on one laptop. RTX 5090, ~9GB VRAM. Silero + faster-whisper + qwen2.5:7b + XTTS-v2, all local. No cloud inference, no API keys, no audio leaving the machine.
